# Research digest: 2026-07-18

Topic requested: dot-com era versus AI era. Trends, similarities, and scenarios built on the
comparison. Pulled by direct web research. Angle note: the consulting-specific story (what
happened to consulting firms in 2000) is the differentiator; every other newsletter will write
the generic bubble comparison.

## The numbers, side by side

1. **Valuation multiples: lower than 2000, but second-highest in 125 years**
   - Source: IntuitionLabs data comparison; multiple corroborating market analyses | 2026
   - Link: https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/ai-bubble-vs-dot-com-comparison
   - Fact: Nasdaq-100 forward P/E hit about 60x in March 2000 versus roughly 26x in 2026. But
     the Shiller CAPE sits near 41 as of May 2026, against about 44 at the 1999 peak, the
     second-highest reading in 125 years of US market data.
   - Why it matters: Both sides of the debate are right on their own metric. Cheaper than 2000
     is true. Second-most-expensive market in history is also true.
   - Uncertain: Secondary aggregation; check against Shiller's own published CAPE data before
     quoting precisely.

2. **Cisco vs Nvidia: the earnings difference**
   - Source: Nasdaq.com analysis; Yahoo Finance | 2026
   - Links: https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/why-nvidia-stock-not-cisco-dot-com-bubble-burst ;
     https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/cisco-took-25-years-investors-203636211.html
   - Fact: Cisco's P/E peaked around 200x in March 2000 (some measures put 1999 at higher).
     Cisco took roughly 25 years to make peak buyers whole. Nvidia trades near 47x with
     $215.9B in FY2026 revenue and real free cash flow. 2000's leaders promised future
     profits; today's leaders book the largest dollar profits in corporate history.
   - Why it matters: The clearest structural difference. The dot-com rally was priced on
     promises. This one is at least partly priced on delivered earnings.
   - Uncertain: A concentration of real earnings can still correct hard; real earnings did not
     save Cisco's buyers from a 25-year round trip.

3. **Capex: this buildout is bigger than the telecom one**
   - Source: IntuitionLabs aggregation of company filings | 2026
   - Link: https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/ai-bubble-vs-dot-com-comparison
   - Fact: Microsoft, Google, Meta, and Amazon are expected to spend about $320B on capex in
     2026, roughly 65 to 70% AI-related. That single year exceeds the cumulative dot-com
     telecom buildout. AI startups took $258.7B of global VC in 2025, 61% of all VC money.
   - Why it matters: The similarity side of the ledger. Infrastructure overbuild funded by
     confident capital is exactly the dot-com pattern, at larger scale.
   - Uncertain: "Exceeds the cumulative telecom buildout" needs an inflation-adjustment check
     before running as a flat fact; attribute to the source.

4. **Private AI valuations, for scale**
   - Source: reported funding rounds, via IntuitionLabs | Feb 2026
   - Link: https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/ai-bubble-vs-dot-com-comparison
   - Fact: OpenAI raised $110B at a $730B pre-money valuation in February 2026. Anthropic
     raised $30B at $380B the same month. Databricks at $134B.
   - Why it matters: Private-market exuberance at a scale the dot-com era never reached.
   - Uncertain: Reported figures, not filings.

## The consulting story (the part nobody else will write)

5. **The Fast Five: the internet won and they still died**
   - Source: Wikipedia (Fast Five, Scient, MarchFirst); Encyclopedia.com | historical
   - Links: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Five_(consulting) ;
     https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scient
   - Fact: Viant, Scient, Razorfish, iXL Enterprises, and MarchFirst were pure-play internet
     consultancies growing revenue about 80% a year with multibillion-dollar market caps by
     2000, positioned as fast alternatives to the Big Five. By December 2000: Viant $63.56 to
     $3.88, Scient $133.75 to $3.03, MarchFirst above $60 to $1.25. MarchFirst went bankrupt
     about a year after it was formed. Scient merged with iXL in 2001 and was bankrupt by July
     2002. Viant was absorbed in 2002. The diversified incumbents survived.
   - Why it matters: This is the whole angle. Being right about the technology (the internet
     was real, it did transform everything) did not protect the specialists priced on that
     being true. What survived was diversified client relationships and cash.
   - Uncertain: Nothing material; well-documented history.

6. **Amazon: what surviving looked like**
   - Source: HBS Online; eWeek retrospective | historical
   - Link: https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/how-amazon-survived-the-dot-com-bubble
   - Fact: Amazon fell from $107 to $7 a share and survived on a working business model,
     using 2000 to 2005 to lay the foundations (including what became AWS and Kindle) while
     competitors died.
   - Why it matters: The crash did not stop the technology. It transferred the technology's
     future to whoever had fundamentals when the money stopped.
   - Uncertain: Retrospective narratives flatter survivors; use as illustration, not proof.

## Pattern

The similarities are on the money side: infrastructure overbuild, capital concentration,
second-highest valuations in history. The differences are on the earnings side: today's
leaders book real profits at roughly half the multiples of 2000's leaders. And the consulting
lesson cuts across both: in 2000 the technology thesis was correct and the pure-play
consultancies still died in about 18 months, while boring incumbents with diversified clients
lived. If a correction comes, it will not settle whether AI is real. It will settle who was
financed on the assumption that nothing would ever pause.
